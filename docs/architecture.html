Verbatim

How Verbatim fits together

A local app that transcribes recorded interviews using context she gives it up front, then asks her to confirm what it isn't sure about. This page shows the parts, the order they run in, and what each one leaves on disk.

Spec v1 · agreed 17 Oct 2026 Version 0.1.2 released Updated 20 Oct · desktop app Mac + Windows · fully local

The system

Everything runs on her computer. Only two things ever cross the boundary: the one-time speech model download during setup, and the update check (which she can switch off) with the signed updates it finds.

The pipeline

One interview goes top to bottom. The left column is what her knowledge base feeds in; the right is what each stage saves, so any stage can be re-run or inspected on its own.

Her side of it

What she actually does, in order, and which steps teach the project something it keeps.

Fitting in 16 GB

Why models load one at a time on her MacBook. The speaker step is measured (about 0.9 GB with TitaNet-Large, on Windows); the rest are estimates.

On disk, and what it's built with

Plain files she could open in any editor, no database, in her data folder (~/Library/Application Support/Verbatim on a Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Verbatim Data on Windows). Each tool has a named fallback in case it disappoints.

Who's speaking: what we measured

Scored on a fully corrected 6:38 sample interview (three speakers, one of them 71% of the words) and on 16 AMI test meetings where the same people return across meetings. The corrected original was never modified; every run used a copy.

  • Name one voice per person; the app does the rest. No grouping setting gives "one voice per person": TitaNet splits each person into several pure fragments, and coarser settings start merging different women. So the app splits finely and recognizes fragments once she names one per person. On two AMI meetings, including one with four women, 4 clicks labelled 79–87% of speech, 95–97% of it correctly. The rest stays unnamed for her to fix.
  • Open question. TitaNet hears the main speaker's words in the sample as two very different voices. Either it's two people, or one person who sounds very different at times. That's being checked by ear.
  • Enroll, don't train. Recognizing a voice means saving its fingerprint once, which takes seconds and changes no model. That's how her own voice and named interviewees work.
  • No fine-tuning for now. One corrected file is too little to train on or test with, most interviewees appear once, and a training pipeline is a maintenance burden. Revisit only if separation stays poor across several of her recordings, starting with the small segmentation model trained on AMI.
  • Planned: learn from her fixes. After she corrects a few stretches, a small classifier on the voice fingerprints relabels the rest of that interview. A first simple version didn't beat automatic detection yet, so it needs her recordings to prove itself.
  • Later idea: a local LLM reads conversational cues (who asks questions, "my sister") to flag likely speaker switches.

What could bite

  • One Mac test round. Speed and memory on her 16 GB MacBook are estimates until it runs. Metal gave nonsense on GitHub's virtual Macs; setup's self-test now falls back to the processor if her graphics chip ever does the same.
  • Similar voices at one table. On the first sample the original voice model did no better than labelling everyone as the main speaker (71% of words). TitaNet-Large reaches 86%. Fast manual fixes stay the fallback.
  • Voiceprints are personal data. Her voice and interviewees' voices stay on her machine, are listed in settings, and can be deleted.
  • Table-mic group audio with strong accents. Context fixes names, not noise.
  • Dolby On's noise reduction sounds clearer to people but could help or hurt transcription. Measured in M2, not assumed.
  • The 1.6 GB model download on hotel Wi-Fi. Setup resumes where it left off (built and tested).
  • No paid code signing. The first open needs "Open Anyway" on a Mac and "Run anyway" on Windows; updates after that are checked against our own signature.
